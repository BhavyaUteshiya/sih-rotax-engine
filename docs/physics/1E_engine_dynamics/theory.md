# Phase 1E: Engine Dynamics & Torque Balance — Theory

## Overview
Phase 1E closes the core causal loop of the engine physics model. It takes the **Indicated Power** generated by the combustion model (Phase 1D) and calculates the resulting angular acceleration and RPM of the engine. Rather than prescribing RPM directly from throttle position or manifold pressure, this phase treats RPM as an **emergent property** arising from the balance of opposing torques acting on the engine's rotating mass.

## Causal Structure
The dynamic state of the engine is governed by Newton's Second Law for Rotation:

$$ \sum \tau = J_{eq} \cdot \alpha $$

Where:
*   $\sum \tau$ is the net torque acting on the system ($Nm$).
*   $J_{eq}$ is the equivalent moment of inertia of the engine, gearbox, and propeller ($kg \cdot m^2$).
*   $\alpha$ is the angular acceleration ($\text{rad}/s^2$).

### Torque Components
The net torque is calculated as the sum of driving torques minus opposing torques:

$$ \tau_{net} = \tau_{indicated} + \tau_{starter} - \tau_{friction} - \tau_{load} $$

1.  **Indicated Torque ($\tau_{indicated}$):** The raw torque produced by combustion, derived from indicated power: $\tau_{indicated} = P_{indicated} / \omega_{engine}$.
2.  **Starter Torque ($\tau_{starter}$):** A discrete torque applied when the electric starter is engaged (active only at low RPM).
3.  **Friction Torque ($\tau_{friction}$):** Internal mechanical and pumping losses of the engine.
4.  **Load Torque ($\tau_{load}$):** The opposing torque exerted by the propeller (mapped through the reduction gearbox back to the engine shaft).

## Internal Friction Model
Since exact mechanical loss data for the Rotax 914 is proprietary, a generalized quadratic friction surrogate is used. Friction torque increases with engine speed:

$$ \tau_{friction} = C_0 + C_1 \cdot \omega_{engine} + C_2 \cdot \omega_{engine}^2 $$

Where $C_0$, $C_1$, and $C_2$ are empirically tuned constants to approximate a ~15-20 kW mechanical loss at maximum rated speed (5800 RPM). A static stiction threshold ensures that friction never drives the engine backward and smoothly brings it to a halt when no driving torque is present.

## Propeller Load Model
The Rotax 914 utilizes a reduction gearbox (ratio ~2.4286:1 or 0.41176 engine-to-propeller speed). The propeller speed $n$ (revs/sec) is thus directly coupled to the engine speed.

The aerodynamic torque demanded by the propeller is described by the standard nondimensional equation:

$$ \tau_{propeller} = C_Q \cdot \rho \cdot n^2 \cdot D^5 $$

Where:
*   $C_Q$: Torque coefficient (dependent on the Advance Ratio $J = v / (n \cdot D)$).
*   $\rho$: Ambient air density (kg/m$^3$).
*   $n$: Propeller rotational speed (rev/s).
*   $D$: Propeller diameter (m).

This aerodynamic torque is then mapped back through the gearbox (accounting for efficiency $\eta_{gearbox}$) to represent the actual $\tau_{load}$ on the engine shaft.

## Zero-RPM Stability & Regularization
A key challenge in rotational dynamics modeling is the singularity at zero RPM (e.g., dividing power by zero to find indicated torque). The model implements explicit mathematical regularization:
*   At very low speeds, indicated torque calculation bypasses the division by zero and returns $0.0$ unless the starter is engaged.
*   Advance ratio calculations cap the denominator to prevent $J \to \infty$ when $n = 0$.
*   A static friction lock engages when speed is near zero and driving torque is insufficient to overcome stiction, hard-clamping $\omega_{engine}$ to $0.0$ to prevent numerical creep.
